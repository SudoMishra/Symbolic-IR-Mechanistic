## Argument map

1. **Opaque backend literals are a transport format, not a reasoning interface.** Stage 1 and the held-out MuSiQue confirmation show large token/latency costs and consistently worse identifier binding.
2. **Citation scores hide two separate failures.** The model can select the right evidence and still fail to reproduce the exact identifier; strict citation accuracy therefore mixes reasoning with serialization.
3. **The tempting extra-reasoning explanation did not replicate.** Stage 5 confirmatory runs found no cross-model evidence that UUIDs systematically buy more hidden reasoning work.
4. **The internal object is more stable than its surface spelling.** Stage 6 geometry and Stage 7 interventions point to shared semantics with a late realization bottleneck.
5. **Typed handles generalize beyond citations.** Stage 8 improves binding in canonicalized person/entity tasks without changing task semantics.
6. **Symbolic IR creates a useful trust boundary, not a complete safety proof.** Stage 9 robustly rejects malformed structure but cannot detect a semantically wrong registered handle on its own.

## Page contract

- The first screen states the thesis in under five seconds and includes a real primary button.
- Every experimental claim is paired with an actual project-generated chart or an explicit numeric receipt.
- The reading sequence alternates compositions: full-field type, split argument, live comparison, full-width proof, paired figures, poster list, inverted close.
- The design remains legible without JavaScript. JavaScript adds only the data control and progressive motion.
- Reduced-motion users receive the complete static page without masked or hidden content.

## Critique log

### Round 1

- The first render exposed overlong display statements, three- and four-line section headings, and a mobile hero wrap.
- Definition labels above large values read as generic eyebrow text rather than evidence.
- Off-screen reveal states made full-page captures appear to contain empty sections.
- Horizontal reveal transforms created measurable overflow.

Changes: shortened headlines, rewrote large statements, turned labels into value-first receipts, kept content visible before intersection events, and removed horizontal transforms.

### Round 2

- Section padding plus figure margins created several long unoccupied stretches.
- Split section introductions put too much vertical distance between the heading and evidence copy.
- The rules list and mobile statistics were too poster-like to remain legible at narrow widths.

Changes: tightened the section rhythm, aligned split introductions, changed mobile statistics to horizontal evidence rows, and reduced mobile list type without weakening the desktop scale.

### Round 3

- The mechanism and boundary introductions still placed all supporting copy in one narrow column.
- The headless inspector reports a 455 px measurement viewport for the configured 390 px render while the CSS layout remains 390 px wide. This creates a false 21 px right-margin discrepancy; the actual screenshot and section geometry use equal 16 px gutters.

Changes: widened the two evidence leads across the grid. The final audit allows only the documented headless `off-centre` measurement discrepancy and exits successfully.

## Long-form revision

The initial visual edition compressed the project to roughly 977 words. This revision restores the engineering argument at article depth: 3,788 rendered words, an eight-part experiment ledger, paired-construction methodology, separate metric definitions, the inconvenient early replication, the held-out result, the negative reasoning-budget result, mechanistic evidence, and the implemented trust boundary.

Editorial references informed the information structure rather than the visual identity: Lilian Weng's motivation-to-taxonomy-to-evidence progression, Sebastian Raschka's baseline-to-experiment-to-caveat pacing, and François Chollet's insistence on defining what a benchmark establishes and what it does not.

### Round 1

- **Seen in one second:** an editorial engineering report about why UUIDs are the wrong model-facing interface, backed by a large experimental program.
